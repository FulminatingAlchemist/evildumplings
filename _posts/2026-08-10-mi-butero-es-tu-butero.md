Do you know the feeling of being so hangry that you no longer want to go anywhere and become grumpier with every passing minute? That's exactly the state my partner was in last Saturday. Instead of trying a new place, which can always be risky, I decided to go to Butero. 
Both the food and the decor can be described as hearty. When you cross the threshold of the small restaurant hidden in a courtyard of the tenement in the Śródmieście district, you are immediately welcomed by an LED sign reading "mi butero es tu butero". A taste of what was to come. The restaurant itself is decorated in an eclectic mix of new and old, perfectly reflecting the spirit of Warsaw. Having lived in Stokes Croft before, I immediately felt at home. For starters, we shared a plate of cheesy chips, served with Polish "Bursztyn" cheese and chives; for mains we had the fried aubergine and the chanterelle pierogi and finished it all off with bilberry pierogi. Not a single dish amazed me, but that's not why I was there. Everything we had was tasty and made us feel better. The staff were very polite and knowledgeable about the menu. The chips were very moreish, and I caught myself going back for more throughout the meal and afterwards.  Both of the mains were decent, although the aubergine could have used a bit more spice; by the end, I found it a little bland. Now the dessert really surprised me; bilberry pierogi are one of my favourite seasonal foods, but Butero's take on them was very unusual. The dish was significantly more savoury than I expected; it was served with a cardamom crumble that gave the whole dish an interesting spin.

Overall, Butero is a safe place that I recommend to anyone feeling a bit down. Next time you are in Warsaw and you are feeling sad, or the weather is just grim, go to Butero; you will feel better, or just go there anyway.
