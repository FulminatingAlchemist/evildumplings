At the time, I was living in Tsukuba, a small, very car-centred city in Japan. I had heard about this place serving "white gyoza", a slight variation on traditional Japanese dumplings with a thicker skin, which are pan-fried. Since driving in Japan seemed like a tall order, I had no other choice but to cycle. After about 45 minutes of cycling and getting lost at least twice, I finally arrived at the place. Too bad it was closed... 
One of the quirks of the restaurant was that it was only open 5 to 7. ( Google Maps now says it has changed to 5:30 to 7:30.) The following week, I decided to go straight after work. I got there at 5:45; the place was absolutely packed. It was an unassuming, boxy building, situated in the suburbs of Tsukuba, surrounded by houses and fields. It was filled with people, a mixture of farm workers who had clearly come straight from the fields, still covered in soil, alongside company workers and families. The small building was filled with smoke from an open-plan kitchen serving essentially the same thing to everyone. Since the menu  had only two food items and drinks: white gyoza and mixed pickles.
One of my favourite things about eating in Japan was osusume (おすすめ), or simply recommendation. You can go to a restaurant, ask what they think is best, and they typically deliver.  There’s a real pride in doing one dish well, so it’s not unusual to find a place that serves only one thing, but you know it's going to be the best version of it you can get.

What you get is a plate of 8 or 16 insanely hot dumplings fried together and filled with a mixture of meat and vegetables. They are served with  vinegar, garlic and chilli on the side. My advice is to poke a hole in every dumpling to let them cool down as much as possible before you eat them; they really are ridiculously hot. 

It's been almost eight months since I moved back from Japan. During my time there I ate everything from takoyaki mixed in a cement mixer, work canteen food, to Japanese takes on Italian food, sushi, snow crab legs and A LOT of ramen. But the one meal I can't stop thinking about is a plate of little unassuming dumplings. That's reason enough for me to go back to Tsukuba.
